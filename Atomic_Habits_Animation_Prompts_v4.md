## HOW THIS VERSION IS DIFFERENT FROM v3

The previous version of this doc had you generating a *second, separate* still
image as an artificial "end state" for every single shot — meaning double the
image generations for no real benefit, since that end-state image never
appeared anywhere else in the video.

This version fixes that. Every clip's first frame and last frame are **both
images you already generated** from the Visual Prompt Sheet. Specifically:

- **CHAIN** — Shot N and Shot N+1 are visually continuous (same subject/scene,
  a state clearly progresses between them — the thermometer rising, the
  cyclist transforming, the calendar filling in). For these, generate ONE clip
  using image N as the first frame and image N+1 as the last frame. Image N+1
  then also becomes the FIRST frame of whatever comes after it — it's doing
  double duty, so you're not paying for it twice.
- **CUT** — Shot N and Shot N+1 are unrelated (different subject, different
  setting, or a narrator beat that doesn't visually continue). For these,
  don't try to morph between them — that would create a distracting, aimless
  warp. Instead, treat image N as its own short clip with **subtle idle
  motion only** (a slow push-in, gentle drift, natural talking-head
  micro-motion), then hard-cut in your editor to the next image's clip.

Net result: **no extra image generations** beyond the 146 stills in the visual
prompt sheet, and every animated transition goes somewhere that actually
appears on screen.

### Quick tally
Out of 145 transitions (shot 2 through shot 146), roughly a third are CHAIN
pairs that become genuine morph clips; the rest are CUT shots that get subtle
idle motion and a hard cut in the edit. Both types are listed below, shot by
shot, so you know exactly what to feed into Kling/Wan for each one.

---

## HOW TO READ EACH ENTRY

- **Type: CHAIN** → First frame = image N. Last frame = image N+1 (already in
  your visual prompt sheet — no new generation needed). Motion prompt
  describes the transformation between them.
- **Type: CUT (idle)** → First frame = image N. This clip has no last-frame
  target; it's a short static-ish clip with idle motion, ending on a hard cut.
  Motion prompt describes the idle movement only.
- **Duration** — suggested clip length in seconds.

General rule: keep CHAIN motion smooth and purposeful — it's moving toward a
real destination. Keep CUT idle motion minimal and calm — a slow push-in,
gentle drift, subtle breathing/blinking on narrator shots — since it's not
building toward anything, just holding the frame alive until the cut.

## GENERAL PACING NOTE

Payoff beats (slightly faster, snappier motion than the surrounding calm pace)
appear at: clips 5→6, 27→28 (tonal shift), 43→44, 45→46, 51→52, 95→96,
124→125, 136→137. These are moments where the narration itself describes a
sudden change or reveal — let them stand out by keeping everything else in the
video calm and understated.

If an image ends up as both a CHAIN endpoint and needs to also idle briefly
before the next cut (marked "(last frame of clip X→Y, see above — also starts
here)" throughout this doc), that idle beat is optional — it exists to give
you breathing room if the narration for that shot runs a bit long, but you can
cut straight from the CHAIN clip to the next clip if the timing is already
tight.
