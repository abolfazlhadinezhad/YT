# ATOMIC HABITS — Visual Prompt Sheet (v5, updated)
### Ready-to-paste image prompts, matched to the v3 narration script
### One image per sentence on longer lines — faster visual pace throughout
### Chalkboard used sparingly (callback moments only), not as a default backdrop
### v4: corrected the [NARRATOR]-shot caption style to exactly match the
### reference thumbnail's 3-line structure (plain / gold accent + underline / plain)

---

## HOW TO USE THIS DOCUMENT

- Every prompt below is written to paste directly into an image generator (ChatGPT Image, Midjourney, etc.).
- **STYLE BLOCK** (below) should be pasted at the start of *every single prompt* — that's what keeps the narrator's face and sweater consistent across dozens of separate generations.
- Prompts are tagged: **[NARRATOR]** = you're in the shot, **[SCENE]** = pure illustration, no narrator, **[CARD]** = text/diagram graphic, no narrator.
- **New in v3 — [CAPTION]** tag: on-screen text baked into the image itself, reinforcing the exact phrase being spoken at that moment. Used selectively — not on every shot. Two different caption styles apply depending on shot type: **[SCENE]/[CARD]** shots use the short lower-third caption style; **[NARRATOR]** shots use the stacked, left-aligned, gold-accent headline style shown in your reference thumbnail (see the two separate "Caption text style" blocks below) — never the lower-third style, since that would compete with the narrator's face.
- This version is **densified**: nearly every sentence now has its own image, so a viewer sees a new picture every 2–4 seconds instead of watching one static shot through a long sentence. Where a shot is a near-duplicate of its neighbor (same subject, small state change), that's intentional — treat it like a mini before/after and let the animation tool handle the transition.
- The **chalkboard** is used only where it earns its place (the "1% Improvement" graph moment). Most other [NARRATOR] and [CARD] shots use floating on-screen graphics against a plain background instead.
- For image-to-image tools: upload your reference photo each time alongside the prompt for best face/outfit consistency, instead of relying on text alone.
- First-frame / last-frame animation prompts for every shot below now live in the companion doc: **Atomic_Habits_Animation_Prompts_v3.md**.

## STYLE BLOCK (paste at the start of every prompt)

```
Semi-realistic soft 3D character-illustration style, like a modern animated
explainer video (think Pixar-adjacent concept art, NOT flat vector/clipart,
NOT a 2D icon set, NOT a flat corporate-deck illustration). Every object and
character must read as having real volume and dimension — soft rounded
3D forms, gentle rim lighting, soft ambient occlusion in the creases and
contact shadows, smooth gradient shading across curved surfaces (not flat
single-tone fills, not hard-edged flat shapes). Subtle glossy/soft-plastic
sheen on solid objects (book covers, coins, glass), subtle translucency and
soft internal light-scatter on the ice cube. Rounded, friendly proportions —
not photorealistic, not exaggerated cartoon, not stylized flat icon art.
Clean even studio lighting, gentle soft-edged shadows (never a hard flat
drop-shadow), no grain or texture noise. Warm color palette: skin tones,
warm browns, olive green, soft golds — set against plain, uncluttered
backgrounds (soft white or a gentle neutral gradient) unless a specific
setting is described. Simple, bold, instantly readable composition,
optimized for a small phone screen. This dimensional soft-3D treatment
applies equally to every element in the frame — the narrator, props,
objects, icons, and diagrams alike — never mix flat 2D icon-style elements
into a shot alongside the softly-shaded 3D narrator.
```

**Recurring narrator character (use this exact description any time [NARRATOR] appears):**

```
A friendly young man in his late 20s, short neatly trimmed dark hair, black
rectangular-frame glasses, short groomed beard and mustache, warm olive skin tone,
wearing a ribbed olive-green turtleneck sweater with sleeves slightly pushed up.
Rendered in the semi-realistic soft 3D character-illustration style described
above — soft dimensional shading and rim lighting on the face, hair, and sweater
knit, not flat vector coloring. Warm, approachable expression, slight friendly
smile.
```

**Optional chalkboard setting** (use only for the moment tagged "chalkboard" below — not a default):

```
A dark green-black chalkboard in a warm wood-grain frame, mounted on a plain wall.
White hand-drawn chalk-style linework and lettering — slightly imperfect, sketchy
chalk texture, bold rounded caps or hand-lettered style. A small chalk eraser
resting on the tray at the bottom.
```

**Default "floating graphic" setup** (use for most [NARRATOR] teaching shots and all [CARD] shots instead of the chalkboard):

```
Simple bold on-screen graphic elements — text, icons, or diagrams — rendered
as clean flat vector shapes, intentionally distinct from the softly-shaded
3D illustration style: these are meant to read as a UI-style overlay
floating in the air beside the narrator, not as part of the illustrated
scene itself, so flat shapes here are correct and not a style violation.
They appear alone on a plain soft-neutral background for text-only cards,
or floating beside the narrator on [NARRATOR] teaching shots. White or dark
charcoal text depending on background contrast, minimal drop shadow for a
subtle floating effect, no chalkboard, no classroom setting.
```

**Caption text style — [CAPTION] on [SCENE] / [CARD] shots** (use whenever [CAPTION] is specified on a scene or card prompt below, no narrator present):

```
A short line of bold, clean sans-serif text baked into the lower third of the
frame, high contrast against the background (white text with a subtle dark
drop shadow, or dark charcoal text on light backgrounds), sized to stay
legible on a small phone screen.
```

**Caption text style — [CAPTION] on [NARRATOR] shots** (use whenever [CAPTION] is specified on a shot where the narrator is on screen — matches the reference thumbnail style, NOT the lower-third style above):

```
Bold stacked headline text positioned in the open negative space beside the
narrator (left side of frame if the narrator stands right-of-center, right
side of frame if he stands left-of-center) — never overlapping his face or
body. Left-aligned, three-line structure exactly matching the reference
thumbnail: a plain lead-in line in dark charcoal/near-black (smaller weight),
then the single most important phrase on its own line set noticeably larger
and bolder in a warm gold-orange accent color, then a short thin horizontal
accent rule (small underline dash) directly beneath that gold line, then a
closing plain line back in dark charcoal/near-black (smaller weight, same as
the lead-in). Clean heavy-weight modern sans-serif, tightly stacked line
spacing, generous negative space around the whole text block — matches the
style of a YouTube-thumbnail headline, not a subtitle bar.
```
